How carbon filters work

Carbon grabs it, and holds on.

Most of Sydney is treated with chloramine. It's built to last the whole way through the pipes, so it's still there at your tap.

Chloramine inWater on
  • It sticks. As water runs through, chlorine and chloramine stick to the surface of the carbon and stay there. That's adsorption.
  • Tiny pores, a huge surface. Activated carbon is full of tiny pores, so even a small cartridge has a huge surface to hold on with.
  • The carbon does it, not the membrane. You can't boil chloramine off or leave it sitting. The membrane doesn't take it out. The carbon does.

Understanding water filtration, the basics

A water filter passes water through stages, and each stage has its own job. Sediment catches the particles. Carbon grabs chlorine, chloramine and the taste and smell they leave. A reverse osmosis membrane purifies. A mineral stage puts the good minerals back.

Sydney's water is safe, but it is not pure. That's where we come in. Sydney Water treats for the city. We purify for your home.

Most of Sydney is treated with chloramine. Chlorine gasses off. Chloramine doesn't. It's built to last the whole way through the pipes, so it's still there at your tap and still there in the shower. You can't boil it off and you can't leave it sitting. The carbon does that work.

Water filtration technology, explained

Every home filter is built from a few technologies, each with its own filtration materials. Sydney Water's treatment makes the water safe for the city. A filter at home is the last step of water treatment, for your house.

  • Mechanical filtration: a sediment cartridge catches silt, dirt and rust by size. Ours range from 5 micron down to 1 micron.
  • Adsorption: activated carbon, as a coconut carbon block, granular carbon or multi stage carbon, holds on to chlorine and chloramine, taste and odour.
  • Membrane filtration: a reverse osmosis membrane, 0.0001 micron, helps remove up to 99% of dissolved contaminants.
  • Remineralisation: calcite and mineral media put calcium, or magnesium and potassium, back after the membrane. Pure Premium adds a ceramic media stage too.

Which technologies a system uses, and in what order, is what sets one water filter apart from another.

How carbon filters work

Carbon works by adsorption. As water runs through, chlorine and chloramine stick to the surface of the carbon and stay there. Activated carbon is full of tiny pores, so even a small cartridge has a huge surface to hold on with.

Every one of our systems uses carbon. Pure Flow is a 0.5 micron carbon block at your own kitchen mixer. Pure Home has a coconut carbon block, then a multi stage carbon cartridge down to 0.22 micron. Our reverse osmosis systems carry a coconut carbon block before the membrane, to protect it, and carbon again at the end for a final taste and odour polish.

The membrane doesn't take chloramine out. The carbon does. That's why the carbon stages matter as much as the membrane.

How reverse osmosis works

Reverse osmosis pushes water through a membrane 0.0001 micron fine, about 700,000 times finer than a hair. Water passes through. Dissolved contaminants are held back and flushed away. It helps remove up to 99% of dissolved contaminants.

The purified water fills a small tank under the sink, so a glass is ready when you want one, and it comes out of its own filtered tap. Then a mineral stage puts minerals back.

  • Pure Essential: four stages. Sediment, coconut carbon, the membrane, then calcium back in for water around pH 7 to 8.
  • Pure Premium: seven stages. The membrane, then magnesium and potassium back in, hydrogen rich and alkaline.
  • Pure Luxe: five quick change stages, with calcium back in and a live purity reading on the display.

How whole house filtration works

Whole house filtration goes in where water enters your home, so every tap, shower, bath and appliance gets filtered water. Pure Home is three stages on a wall bracket: a grooved sediment cartridge, a coconut carbon block, then multi stage carbon down to 0.22 micron.

Pure Home Reserve is the same three stage filtration inside a lockable stainless cabinet in 22 colours. Both keep calcium and magnesium in.

Disinfectants can be harsh on skin, especially in a hot shower. Ours helps reduce that. Chlorine and chloramine can be hard on hair over time. Same reason swimmers get that dry, brassy look.

What is the difference between a carbon filter and reverse osmosis?

Carbon grabs chlorine, chloramine and the taste and smell they leave. Reverse osmosis adds a 0.0001 micron membrane that helps remove up to 99% of dissolved contaminants, then puts minerals back. Our reverse osmosis systems use both: carbon before and after the membrane.

Does a water filter take the minerals out?

Whole house filtration keeps calcium and magnesium in. Reverse osmosis helps remove up to 99% of dissolved contaminants, then puts minerals back: calcium in Pure Essential and Pure Luxe, magnesium and potassium in Pure Premium. Minerals are good, so we keep them.

Should I filter the whole house or just the kitchen?

Whole house filtration filters every tap, shower and appliance, down to 0.22 micron, and keeps calcium and magnesium in. Reverse osmosis purifies the water you drink at the kitchen, through a 0.0001 micron membrane. Have one, or have both. Compare the two.

How often are the cartridges changed?

Once a year. The professional yearly service and Filtered for Life change every cartridge and check the system. The DIY kit posts the year's cartridges to you.

How fine does each system filter?

Pure Flow's carbon block is 0.5 micron. Pure Home and Pure Home Reserve filter down to 0.22 micron. The reverse osmosis membrane in Pure Essential, Pure Premium and Pure Luxe is 0.0001 micron. A human hair is about 70 micron. Micron figures as published by the manufacturers.

Is there a water filter that removes herbicides?

Sydney Water tests for pesticides, the group that includes herbicides, at customers' taps, and its quarterly reports list them as not detected in every delivery system. Activated carbon can help with some herbicides, but Australia's drinking water guidelines note that adsorption to carbon can be incomplete, so we don't sell any filter as a complete herbicide fix. Our carbon stages are there to help reduce chlorine and chloramine, taste and odour.

Is Sydney tap water safe to drink?

Yes. Sydney's water is safe, but it is not pure. That's where we come in. Sydney Water treats for the city. We purify for your home. Most of Sydney is treated with chloramine. You can't boil it off and you can't leave it sitting. Carbon helps reduce it.
