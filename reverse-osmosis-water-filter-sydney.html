What reverse osmosis removes, and what it does not

Our reverse osmosis systems help remove up to 99% of dissolved contaminants. The membrane works down to about 0.0001 micron, which helps reduce heavy metals, nitrates, PFAS and dissolved salts, and helps remove microplastics. Sydney's water is safe to drink. Sydney Water publishes what is in the supply, and it is treated for a whole city. Reverse osmosis is for what is left once it is safe: the taste, and the dissolved things a carbon filter alone does not touch.

One honest line. The membrane doesn't take chloramine out. The carbon does. Chloramine is the disinfectant Sydney Water uses across most of the city, and for that the answer is whole house filtration at the mains. Plenty of homes run both: the whole house system for every tap and shower, and a reverse osmosis system for the water they drink.

How a reverse osmosis water filter works, stage by stage

Mains water comes into the cabinet and passes through the stages in order. Five stage reverse osmosis. That's purified water on tap. No more bottles coming through the front door.

  • Sediment. A five micron spun cartridge takes the grit, rust and sand out first.
  • Carbon block. Coconut carbon helps reduce the chlorine, and with it the taste and the smell.
  • The membrane. Water is pushed under pressure through a membrane with openings around 0.0001 micron. PFAS, microplastics, dissolved metals and salts are rejected and flushed to the drain. The purified water is stored in a small pressure tank beside the system.
  • Alkaline. The good minerals go back in on the way to the tap. We remineralise, because minerals are good water.
  • Carbon polish. One last pass before the tap.

Pure Essential runs four: the same sediment, carbon and membrane, then calcium and a coconut carbon polish in one finishing stage, for a neutral pH. Pure Premium adds stages after the membrane, a hydrogen rich alkaline stage, a ceramic stage and an inline conditioner, which is where its seven come from. Pure Luxe runs five in quick change cartridges inside a compact sealed case, with a booster pump, a leak sensor and a display that shows the TDS reading live.

Pure Essential, Pure Plus, Pure Premium and Pure Luxe reverse osmosis systems compared

Pure Essential is the neutral one at $1,180 installed: four stages, the same membrane, then calcium back in for a gentler pH, designed for about pH 7 to 8, on its own chrome filtered tap.

Pure Plus is the alkaline starting point at $1,280 installed: five stages, the membrane, and alkaline mineral rich water on its own dedicated drinking tap. It is the under sink reverse osmosis filter most people picture, and it purifies the water the same way the others do.

Pure Premium adds hydrogen richness and fuller remineralisation at $1,590 installed. Seven stages: the same purification, then a hydrogen rich alkaline stage, a ceramic stage and an inline conditioner. It is the one we compare against a year of bottled water above.

Pure Luxe adds live TDS monitoring in a compact smart unit at $2,290 installed. Quick change cartridges, a booster pump, a leak sensor and a display on the front that shows the TDS reading as you pour. The same membrane, with calcium back in to help balance the pH.

All four are WaterMark certified systems, with filter media compliant to NSF/ANSI standards. What does reverse osmosis remove?

Our reverse osmosis systems help remove up to 99% of dissolved contaminants. The membrane works down to about 0.0001 micron, which helps reduce heavy metals, nitrates, PFAS and dissolved salts. The membrane does not take the disinfectant chloramine out, and for that you want the whole house system at the mains.

Does reverse osmosis remove the good minerals too?

No. The membrane does the deep separation first, then the system puts minerals back. Pure Essential adds calcium for a neutral finish, Pure Plus finishes alkaline with magnesium, Pure Premium adds hydrogen richness with magnesium and potassium, and Pure Luxe adds calcium and shows the TDS reading live. Minerals are good water. We take the contaminants out, not the good part.
