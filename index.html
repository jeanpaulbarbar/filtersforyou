        Whole house or under the sink?

        They do different jobs. A whole house system sits at the mains, where water enters the home, so every tap, shower, bath and the washing machine runs filtered. Ours filter down to 0.22 micron with a sediment stage and two stages of carbon, and help reduce chlorine and chloramine, as published by the manufacturer. Calcium and magnesium stay in.

        A reverse osmosis system sits under the kitchen sink and purifies the water you drink and cook with. Its membrane works at about 0.0001 micron, around 700,000 times finer than a hair, and helps remove up to 99% of dissolved contaminants. Then a finish goes back in, neutral, alkaline or hydrogen rich. Plenty of Sydney homes run both, which is what the Deluxe packages are. Read more on whole house filtration and reverse osmosis.

        Should I filter the whole house or just the kitchen?

        They do different jobs. A whole house system at the mains filters every tap, shower, bath and appliance, and helps reduce chlorine and chloramine.         Is Sydney tap water safe to drink?

        Sydney tap water is treated to meet the Australian Drinking Water Guidelines, so it is safe to drink when it leaves the plant. What reaches your tap can still carry a chlorine taste, sediment picked up from older pipes, and in some suburbs PFAS. A filter deals with what is left after the treatment plant has done its job, which is why the water tastes different out of a filtered tap.

        What is the difference between a water filter and a water purifier?

        Most people mean the same thing by both words. In the trade a water filter strains contaminants out through a cartridge, and a water purifier usually means reverse osmosis, which pushes the water through a membrane fine enough to take out dissolved solids as well. Every system we install does one or the other, and the reverse osmosis systems do both.
